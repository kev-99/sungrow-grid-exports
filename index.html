Grid export findings

Three findings from the site's own iSolarCloud data. Two were invisible until per-phase meter data was exported, and they affect both systems. This sheet does not cover the System 2 disconnection events — those are documented separately.

FINDING 1

Both systems export on one phase while importing on another — and the net meter reading shows nothing

Across 17 days of per-phase meter data, one phase is exporting more than 0.5 kW while another imports more than 0.5 kW on 25.8% of intervals on System 1 and 26.0% on System 2. The net grid figure — the only figure available before this data was exported — reads close to zero through most of it. Over the 17 days the two system meters between them recorded 489 kWh of per-phase export, of which 319 kWh, 65%, appears in no net figure anywhere.

FINDING 2

It happens every day, and phase C — the least-loaded phase on both systems — takes half the export

The behaviour appears on every one of the 17 days measured, between 8% and 37% of intervals. Phase loading is markedly uneven in the same direction on both systems. If the inverter spreads its AC output evenly across the three phases while the load sits mostly on A and B, the lightly loaded phase exports — which is what the data shows, though as a tendency across all three phases rather than a phase-C-only effect.

FINDING 3

System 2 exports from its battery after dark and at very low state of charge

Over 1 July – 6 August, System 2 exported to the grid with the array producing nothing, and exported while its battery was nearly empty — roughly twice System 1 by count, and more than twice by energy. Each event is short and the energy is bought back within hours. This is raised as a configuration symptom rather than a cost claim: the energy involved is small, but discharging a nearly empty battery to the grid also bears on battery cycling and warranty, and it is behaviour Sungrow's own guidance does not describe.

System 1 System 2

The clearest single case — 16 July, 15:45 to 15:55. Array producing about 1 kW. Battery at 13% state of charge. The inverter delivered 10.4 kW then 16.1 kW of AC output, exporting 9.8 kW and then 7.6 kW of it — then reversed to absorbing 16.1 kW five minutes later. State of charge fell from 19% to the 6% floor within the hour. Meter voltage was normal throughout (246.6–247.9 V). No fault was logged and no notification was issued.

How these figures were verified, and one thing worth knowing. Phase A + B + C reconciles with the net grid channel to a median of 60 W — one meter quantum — so the two are on the same basis. Grid voltage was normal at every event listed here, so none of this is a supply disturbance. These figures are measured at each system's own energy meter; what crosses the site boundary depends on how the two systems' simultaneous flows combine, and on how the retail meter totals the phases (question 4). And the inverter's own “Feed-in power” channel — a net figure, behaving as one — does not register per-phase export: of the 652 System 1 and 732 System 2 intervals with real per-phase export while the net reads ≈ 0, it reports export in 11 and 10 respectively. This behaviour will therefore not appear in the inverter's feed-in energy registers, or in any iSolarCloud export figure derived from them.
WHAT WE ARE ASKING

Seven questions

  1. Is “Feed-In Limitation for Each Phase” enabled on either inverter, and at what value? We understand it is visible only through Local Access in the app, not the WiNet interface. Sungrow · Installer
  2. Does the SH20T balance its AC output evenly across the three phases, or match each phase to its own load? If evenly, what is the intended behaviour on a site with deliberately uneven phase loading? Sungrow
  3. Why does phase C carry roughly a sixth of the load of phases A and B on both systems? Is the phase allocation across the switchboard and the two SolShare units as designed? Installer
  4. Is the site metered net across the three phases, or per phase? This determines whether Finding 1 is a cost or a compliance matter, and we cannot size it until we know. Installer
  5. Sungrow's own guidance states that a correctly installed and configured system does not discharge the battery to the grid, and describes only momentary transients. How is a ~10-minute discharge of 10–16 kW from a battery at 11–13% state of charge, followed by a full reversal, reconciled with that? Sungrow
  6. Please confirm the energy meter CT polarity and phase assignment on both systems, using Sungrow's documented test — with PV off, apply a known load one phase at a time and confirm the portal shows import, not export. Installer
  7. Given that the inverter's own Feed-in power channel does not register per-phase export, how would Sungrow expect an owner to detect this behaviour from the portal at all? Sungrow
SCOPE AND LIMITS

What this sheet does not claim

Findings 1 and 2 are measured over 17 consecutive midwinter days, not a season — they should be re-measured in spring before being treated as annual figures. They affect both systems roughly equally by count, and are larger on System 1 by energy and at higher magnitudes, so they are a site-wide characteristic and are not presented as a System 2 defect. No cost figure is given: that depends on whether the retail meter nets across phases or bills per phase, which is question 4. Most Australian polyphase interval meters do sum the three phases algebraically; if this site's meter does too, the billing impact of Finding 1 is small, and its significance lies instead in per-phase export limits (Ausgrid's limits apply per phase), voltage on the lightly loaded phase, and the fact that no figure available to the owner reflects the true flows. An earlier three-day version of this sheet quoted 21% / 16% and a 59–70% phase-C share; those figures are superseded by the ones here. Nothing identifies a root cause — these are measurements and questions.
